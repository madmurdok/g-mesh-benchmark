Summary

G-mesh reduces token usage by 8.1% across 25 tasks compared to baseline grep/Read/Glob approaches, with a paired reduction of 6.9% over 71 matched comparisons. Oracle pass rates were similar, with g-mesh at 74/75 (99%) and baseline at 72/74 (97%).

However, the results have important caveats. Only 4 of 12 tasks with declared expected winners matched the observed lower-token arm, suggesting the tool's efficiency is task-dependent and difficult to predict upfront. Three tasks marked as "parity" showed unexpectedly large asymmetries ranging from 29% to 44% difference, warranting closer investigation of when g-mesh actually outperforms.

A critical finding emerged from self-verification analysis: while trusting g-mesh without manual re-verification saves 15.2% of tokens, oracle accuracy drops from 99% to 97%, with the tool missing real errors that verification catches. This suggests adopting g-mesh requires either accepting slightly lower reliability or continuing to re-verify results, which partially offsets the token savings.

For adoption, g-mesh offers modest but real efficiency gains with strong baseline pass rates, but success depends on understanding which task types benefit most and whether your accuracy requirements justify the verification overhead. The unconditional 8.1% savings may not apply uniformly across your codebase.
