Summary

Across 20 tasks, g-mesh achieved a higher oracle pass rate (97% compared to baseline's 90%) but used approximately 8.7% more tokens unconditionally. In paired task comparisons, g-mesh used 3.5% fewer tokens while maintaining its accuracy advantage.

Expected-winner predictions aligned with observed results in 6 of 8 cases, suggesting reasonable benchmark calibration. One outlier task showed unexpected token asymmetry despite being labeled for parity.

The most revealing finding concerns g-mesh's internal verification cost. When configured to trust g-mesh results without re-verification, token consumption dropped 34.6% (from 5.7M to 3.7M tokens), but oracle pass rate also fell from 97% to 95%. This one-task accuracy loss confirms that manual re-verification catches genuine g-mesh mistakes rather than representing unnecessary caution. The cost of verification is substantial but justified.

In practical terms: g-mesh trades a modest overall token increase for meaningfully better accuracy. The verification overhead—while significant—is necessary to catch real errors. For developers prioritizing correctness over token efficiency, g-mesh's higher pass rate justifies the added cost.
