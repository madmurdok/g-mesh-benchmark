Summary

This benchmark compared g-mesh to traditional grep/Read/Glob navigation on one representative task. Both approaches completed successfully with 100% oracle pass rates. G-mesh used 64,529 tokens on average versus 66,934 for the baseline, yielding a 3.6% token reduction. The paired comparison confirmed this efficiency gain at the same level.

The benchmark's expected-winner analysis validated the test design: the observed lower-token arm matched the declared expectation, indicating well-calibrated methodology. The token savings reflect g-mesh's ability to resolve ambiguous symbols, cross-file impacts, and call-graph queries directly, avoiding the multiple round-trips and manual verification that grep and Read require for complex questions.

However, the findings rest on a single task. A 3.6% reduction is consistent but modest and may vary with code structure and query patterns. The equal pass rates across both approaches confirm neither introduces correctness risks—both reliably navigate the codebase.

For your decision: adopt g-mesh if your workflow heavily involves ambiguous symbols, multi-file impact analysis, or call-graph questions where you'd otherwise grep iteratively or manually verify results. If most navigation is straightforward single-symbol lookups, the efficiency gain may not justify the tool overhead. The savings are real but incremental; the primary benefit is reduced cognitive load on complex queries rather than dramatic token reduction.
