Summary

This benchmark evaluated g-mesh against a baseline approach (grep/Read/Glob) on a single feature-request categorized task. The results show a clear tradeoff between efficiency and effectiveness.

G-mesh demonstrated substantial token economy, consuming 156,867 tokens compared to the baseline's 324,089 tokens—a 51.6% reduction. This efficiency advantage suggests that g-mesh's structured code navigation approach requires fewer tool calls and less exploratory work to gather context.

However, this efficiency came at the cost of task completion. The baseline approach achieved a 100% oracle pass rate, successfully completing the feature-request task, while g-mesh failed to pass, scoring 0 out of 1. This 100-point divergence in pass rates within the feature-request category indicates the baseline was substantially more effective for this type of work.

The single-task sample size is small and limits the generalizability of these findings. The feature-request category may present specific challenges to g-mesh's approach—perhaps requiring broader context gathering or exploration patterns that g-mesh doesn't optimize for. Conversely, the baseline's exploratory nature, despite higher token cost, may have enabled more thorough investigation necessary to solve feature-request tasks successfully.

For teams prioritizing token efficiency, g-mesh offers clear value. However, teams where task completion rates are paramount may find the baseline's reliability more important than its higher token consumption, particularly for feature-request work.
