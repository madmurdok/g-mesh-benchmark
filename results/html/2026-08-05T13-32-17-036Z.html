Summary

This benchmark evaluated g-mesh against the baseline approach of using grep, Read, and Glob tools across three representative code-navigation tasks. Both approaches achieved perfect accuracy on all tasks tested, with a 100 percent oracle pass rate for each arm.

G-mesh showed a consistent but modest token advantage over the baseline. Across the three tasks, the g-mesh-configured approach used an average of 196,435 tokens compared to 202,064 tokens for the baseline, representing a 2.8 percent reduction in token consumption. This reduction held consistently when comparing paired measurements across the three tasks.

The expected-winner analysis found that all three tasks performed as predicted: each task's declared expected winner matched the arm that actually consumed fewer tokens, suggesting the token advantage was both measurable and aligned with initial expectations.

The data shows that g-mesh and the baseline are functionally equivalent for these tasks, both achieving complete accuracy. The token savings from g-mesh, while real and consistent, are modest—less than three percent. Adoption would depend on whether this efficiency gain and g-mesh's additional capabilities justify integration and maintenance overhead in your specific workflow.
