# g-mesh 2.8.1 in session mode: the crossover exists, and it is at question 17

**Run**: `results/session-economy/2026-08-20T13-49-21-935Z.json` — 135 records, 123 `ok`,
12 `skipped`, 0 `error`.
**Under test**: g-mesh 2.8.1, bench 0.20.0.
**Invocation**: `G_MESH_BENCH_SESSION_REPS=low npm run session-economy` — one chain per
(corpus, arm), arms `gmesh-configured`, `serena-configured`, `baseline`, both corpora.
**Spend**: $16.33. **Wall clock**: ~1h20m.

## Read this limitation first

`low` repetitions means **one chain per (corpus, arm)**. Every position on every curve below is a
single call. There is no within-cell variance to quote, and no per-position difference is worth
reading on its own. What a single chain can establish is *shape* — the direction a curve moves over
twenty-odd positions — and that is all this report claims.

The 12 skipped records are the `implementation` tasks, skipped symmetrically across all three arms
(3 excalidraw, 1 task-tracker each). They bias nothing, but they mean the chains are 30 and 11
positions long rather than 33 and 12.

## 1. What this overturns

`docs/results/v0.2.0-session-economy-findings.md` concluded, in its own words, that a real session
does **not** amortize the schema tax "for the majority case". Its evidence was `lookup`:

| | v0.2.0 (session) | v0.2.0 (isolated) | **this run (session)** |
|---|---|---|---|
| g-mesh vs baseline, `lookup` | **+66.9%** | +45.6% | **+7.0%** |

Session mode used to make g-mesh's disadvantage on the majority category *worse* than running the
same questions in isolation. It no longer does — the penalty is now a twentieth of what it was, and
`lookup` is the largest category in the registry.

**This is not a controlled comparison.** v0.2.0 ran a different g-mesh (0.x), a different bench
(0.2.0), two arms, two chains, and a task registry that has since grown. The number to carry forward
is not "+66.9% became +7.0% because of change X" — it is that the conclusion built on the old figure
no longer describes the tool, and any document repeating it should be re-checked.

## 2. The crossover, on the long corpus

Cumulative tokens through position, g-mesh against baseline, excalidraw (30 scored positions):

| through position | 1 | 5 | 10 | 12 | 13 | 16 | **17** | 24 | 28 | 33 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| g-mesh vs baseline | +67% | +60% | +78% | +43% | +6% | +1% | **−2%** | −5% | −11% | **−9%** |

A session pays a real premium for g-mesh early — it is 54–80% more expensive cumulatively through
the first eleven questions — and gets it back if the session keeps going. The sign flips at
**position 17**, and every position from 17 to 33 stays negative, ending at −9% cumulative.

Per-chain totals say the same thing: median 200,742 tokens against baseline's 253,086 (−21%), and
$3.11 against $4.12 (−25%).

**What is robust and what is not.** The direction is robust: seventeen consecutive positions on the
negative side is not one lucky call. The exact index is not — the collapse from +43% to +6% happens
across positions 12–13 on the strength of two individual calls (baseline spends 317k and 299k there;
g-mesh 213k and 73k). Read it as "somewhere in the mid-teens", not as 17.

## 3. Why the short corpus does not cross over

task-tracker-mcp is 11 scored positions and never reaches the flip:

| through position | 1 | 4 | 5 | 8 | 12 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| g-mesh vs baseline | −17% | −9% | +4% | +2% | **+14%** |

Interestingly it starts *ahead* — g-mesh is cheaper through the first four questions — then loses
ground and ends +14%. So the curve is not simply "expensive first, cheap later"; on a small codebase
the early questions are ones g-mesh answers well, and the chain ends before the amortisation that
rescues the long corpus can happen.

The practical reading, which is what a user needs: **g-mesh's session economics depend on the
session being long against a large codebase.** Eleven questions about a small project is the shape
where it costs more, and that shape is common.

## 4. The structural fact from v0.2.0 still holds

v0.2.0's mechanism — `cacheRead` grows with position because a longer conversation is re-read in
full on every turn — is unchanged. Every arm's curve grows: on excalidraw, baseline goes from 24.8k
at position 1 to 482.0k at 33; g-mesh from 32.2k to 402.6k.

What changed is the *slope*. g-mesh's re-read grows more slowly through the tail, which is the whole
mechanism behind section 2: by position 28 it reads 453k against baseline's 841k. The tax did not
amortize away — g-mesh simply stopped adding to it as fast as grep-driven exploration does.

## 5. Category shape in session mode

Mean total tokens, both corpora pooled:

| category | baseline | g-mesh | Δ |
|---|---|---|---|
| multi-hop | 319,255 | 213,416 | **−33%** |
| feature-request | 284,983 | 256,138 | −10% |
| semantic-search | 360,387 | 327,152 | −9% |
| ambiguous-name | 219,644 | 209,944 | −4% |
| scenario | 167,191 | 169,741 | +2% |
| lookup | 102,949 | 110,201 | +7% |
| control | 123,209 | 190,561 | **+55%** |

Same shape as isolated mode: g-mesh wins where the answer is a graph walk and loses where one grep
suffices. The token-economy report's caution about category cells applies here with more force, not
less — these are single-chain means.

## 6. What this run does not establish

- **Anything about variance.** One chain per cell. A `normal`-repetition run (two chains) is what
  would let any of these numbers carry an error bar; it costs roughly 8 hours.
- **That 2.8.1 caused the change against v0.2.0.** Too much moved in between. What would isolate it
  is a chain on an older g-mesh against today's registry, which nothing here does.
- **Serena as a fair third point.** It is in the tables (its excalidraw chain costs $6.09 against
  g-mesh's $3.11) but its arm has been re-worked since the runs it is being compared with; treat it
  as context, not as a controlled comparison.
