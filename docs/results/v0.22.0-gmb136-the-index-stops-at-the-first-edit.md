# GMB-136: the index stops at the first edit

**Date:** 2026-08-24
**Conclusion:** the g-mesh arm does not "abandon the index" — it uses it to navigate and drops it to
verify. Native-tool share collapses at the first `Edit` in **both sweeps independently** — 64%→0%
and 29%→3% — and what replaces it is `grep releaseTask`, which is `find_references`' exact question.

> **Amended 2026-08-24 (GMB-138).** The first version of this doc pooled both sweeps into one
> 6-rep sample and claimed the arm "doubles baseline's verification work". The collapse survives
> that split; the doubling does not. Corrected in place — see *Per sweep*, below.
>
> **Amended again 2026-08-24 (GMB-139) — this doc's premise is retracted.** A third independent
> sweep of this task has g-mesh as the *cheapest and fastest* arm (8, 11, 13 turns against
> baseline's 18, 11, 18, oracle 3/3). Measured across 18 sweeps instead of one, g-mesh's gap on this
> task is **+8.1% turns and +47% tokens**, of which the static prefix explains **76%** — so the
> opening claim below, that this is a behaviour failure rather than the prefix tax, is **wrong**.
> What survives is the phase observation in *Per sweep*: post-edit native share is low in all three
> sweeps. What does not survive is any cost consequence attached to it — the grep storm appears in
> two sweeps of three. Full numbers in `v0.22.0-gmb139-post-edit-cue-ab.md` §4–5. Read that first.

**Data:** 18 saved transcripts of `tt-implement-release-cancelled-task-bug` — three arms × three
repetitions × **two independent sweeps** (`results/transcripts/2026-08-23T23-52-56-121Z/` and
`2026-08-24T09-34-55-557Z/`), so six repetitions per arm. No new benchmark run was needed or paid
for; both sweeps already ran with `G_MESH_BENCH_SAVE_TRANSCRIPTS=yes`.

## Why this task was worth opening

On the 2026-08-24 sweep it alone accounts for **41%** of `gmesh-configured`'s total token overspend
against baseline (+556,017 of +1,359,002 gross), taking 21.3 turns against baseline's 11.5. Since
cost tracks turns almost linearly, one task behaving badly moves the whole headline.

> **That was one sweep's draw.** This task's historical spread is n=57 runs, mean 15.1 turns,
> sd 3.5, **CV 23%** — a three-rep sweep of it reverses its own headline between runs, and did so
> twice within 24 hours. The 21.3-turn sweep sits +3 SE above the mean; the sweep that followed sits
> −2.2 SE below it. Nothing in this section should be read as a property of the task or the arm.
> See GMB-139 §4.

## The split is at the first edit, not at a failed lookup

Counting every search call in the 18 transcripts, partitioned at each run's first `Edit`/`Write`:

| arm | reps | before first edit | after first edit |
|---|---|---|---|
| `gmesh-configured` | 6 | 15 native / 35 total (**43%**) | 1 native / 50 total (**2%**) |
| `serena-configured` | 6 | 31 native / 63 total (49%)\* | 3 native / 35 total (9%) |
| `baseline` | 6 | 0 / 29 | 0 / 26 |

### Per sweep

Pooling hides that the two sweeps differ. Split:

| sweep | arm | pre-edit | post-edit | post-edit greps |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2026-08-23 | `gmesh-configured` | 9/14 (**64%**) | **0/18 (0%)** | 10 |
| 2026-08-23 | `baseline` | 0/16 | 0/15 | 3 |
| 2026-08-24 | `gmesh-configured` | 6/21 (**29%**) | **1/32 (3%)** | 15 |
| 2026-08-24 | `baseline` | 0/13 | 0/11 | 4 |

**The collapse is the robust part.** Two independent sweeps, and post-edit native share is 0% and
3% against a pre-edit 64% and 29%. That is the finding.

**"Doubles baseline's verification work" is not, and is retracted.** Against baseline's own
post-edit count it is 18 vs 15 (**1.2×**) on 08-23 and 32 vs 11 (**2.9×**) on 08-24. The pooled
50-vs-26 figure above is carried by one sweep and should not be quoted as a general ratio.

\* Serena's pre-edit native count includes 6 `activate_project` and 5 `initial_instructions` calls,
which are session setup rather than search. Excluding them its pre-edit share is 20/52 (38%), and
the before/after contrast is the same shape as g-mesh's, one step weaker.

Tool-by-tool, after the first edit:

| arm | calls |
|---|---|
| `gmesh-configured` | `Grep`×25, `Read`×20, `Glob`×4, `find_references`×1 |
| `serena-configured` | `Read`×15, `Grep`×15, `get_symbols_overview`×2, `Glob`×2, `find_symbol`×1 |
| `baseline` | `Read`×16, `Grep`×7, `Glob`×3 |

Two things fall out. The navigation phase is healthy — g-mesh reaches for `search_code` (×11),
`find_callers` (×3) and `find_references` (×1) before touching the code, and gets to the same root
cause baseline finds. The verification phase is where it doubles baseline's work (50 calls against
26) using almost nothing but text search.

## What it greps for

Of the 25 post-edit greps, the overwhelming majority are the literal symbol it just changed —
`releaseTask` or `releaseTask|release_task` — issued three to four times per run. The shape is
identical across repetitions and across both sweeps. From
`…-gmesh-configured-rep1.ndjson` (2026-08-24), immediately after the fix and the regression test
were written:

> **say:** Now let's run the tests to confirm the fix works and nothing else broke.
> → `Glob {"pattern":"package.json"}`
> → `Grep {"pattern":"releaseTask|release_task", "path":"…/tests", "output_mode":"files_with_matches"}`
> ← `Found 3 files tests/lifecycle.test.ts tests/ownership.test.ts tests/mcp-smoke.test.ts`
> → `Grep {"pattern":"releaseTask|release_task", "path":"…/tests/ownership.test.ts", "-C":5}`
> → `Grep {"pattern":"releaseTask|release_task", "path":"…/tests/mcp-smoke.test.ts", "-C":5}`
>
> **say:** None of these touch cancelled tasks with `releaseTask`, so the fix doesn't affect them.

That is "which files reference this symbol, and what do they do with it" — three grep round trips
where `find_references("releaseTask")` is one, and whose `files` array the guidance doc explicitly
says to answer this from.

## The likely cause: every trigger in the guidance doc fires before the edit

`GMESH_CONFIGURED_CLAUDE_MD` (12,881 B) is written entirely in the imperative of navigation and
pre-change impact analysis. Its triggers, verbatim:

- "prefer g-mesh … for **cross-file impact analysis**, ambiguous naming … call-graph/multi-hop questions"
- "`find_references` … every usage of a symbol across the project; **use before renaming or removing** something"
- "`get_dependencies` … use for **impact analysis before changing** a shared module"
- "`search_code` … Default to this as your ***first* move** on a 'find the function/bug that does X' prompt"

Every one of them is a *pre-change* cue: before renaming, before changing, first move. Nothing in
the document addresses the state the agent is actually in after line 4 of its own diff — "I have
changed something; what did I affect?" — even though that is the same question, and the document
answers it well two bullets further down for the pre-change case.

**This is a hypothesis, not a finding.** The correlation is sharp and reproduces across two sweeps,
but the doc's wording is not the only candidate: post-edit context is also longer and more
edit-focused, which may bias tool choice on its own. The experiment that would settle it is one
sentence added to the guidance covering the post-change case, benchmarked on this task at three
repetitions — with the native/total split above as the metric, not tokens.

**Do not simply add that sentence and ship it.**
`v0.20.0-guidance-doc-pays-for-itself-findings.md` is the precedent: trimming this same document by
57% saved 2,776 tokens a turn and cost more than that back in extra turns, because the agent started
calling `find_definition` before the tool it actually wanted. Wording changes here are behavioural
changes and have to be measured as such.

## Scope: proven for this task, untested beyond it

The registry cannot currently answer whether this generalises, and that is itself worth recording:

| task | category | post-edit phase? |
|---|---|---|
| `tt-implement-release-cancelled-task-bug` | implementation | yes — 50 calls, the pattern above |
| `ex-implement-mutateelement-elbow-zero-position` | implementation | **no** — 0 calls after the edit; the arm stops |
| `tt-feature-bulk-cancel-epic-tasks` | feature-request | **not applicable** — "describe how you'd implement it", judge-graded, no edits at all |

So exactly one task in the registry has a substantial verify-after-edit phase, and it is the one
that shows the collapse. The claim "implementation-category-wide" is **not supported** — there is no
second task to support it with. A registry gap, not a null result: an implementation task that
requires checking the blast radius after a change would test this directly.

## The two sweeps ran identical code — this is not a version regression

Worth stating plainly, because the numbers invite the opposite reading. Turn counts on this task:

| sweep | `gmesh-configured` | `baseline` |
|---|---|---|
| 2026-08-23 | 14, 12, 17 | 13, 13, 17 |
| 2026-08-24 | **23, 25, 16** | **10, 11, 12** |

g-mesh went above its entire historical band on this task (10–18 turns across roughly fifteen
sweeps since 2026-08-02) while baseline dropped to the bottom of its own. **`baseline` contains no
g-mesh**, so whatever moved it was not the tool — which rules out the tool having moved g-mesh's
arm either, by the same logic that makes baseline a control at all.

And the binary did not change between them. The g-mesh binary was rebuilt at 02:51 on 08-24,
between the two sweeps, which looks like a confound until you check what it built:
`git diff bdf6849 ec9ee8d --stat` is **empty** — that commit is a release-branch merge into main
that changed no code. Same tree, both sweeps.

So the sweep-to-sweep swing on this task is **unexplained**, and stays unexplained here. Three
repetitions per sweep cannot separate a real environmental difference from a high-variance task,
and this task is visibly high-variance: its historical per-rep range is 10 to 25 turns.

**A gap this exposed:** establishing "same code, both sweeps" took a binary mtime, a commit log and
a tree diff, because **a token-economy run record carries no g-mesh version**. It records
`corpusRevision` and `serenaRevision` but not the version of the thing under test. Filed as part of
GMB-138.

## What this does not say

It does not move the sweep's headline. This is one task of 45, and the systematic prefix cost —
8,762 tokens per turn, paid whether the arm uses its tools or not — is a separate, already-quantified
thing. The overlap is only that both are paid per turn, so a phase that doubles the turn count pays
the prefix twice over.
