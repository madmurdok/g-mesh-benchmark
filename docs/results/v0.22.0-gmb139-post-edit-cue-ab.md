# GMB-139: a post-edit cue in the guidance doc — did not resolve, and the control moved the bigger result

**Date:** 2026-08-24
**Verdict on the hypothesis:** **not supported.** The sentence did not raise post-edit native tool
use — one native call either side. But at three repetitions on a task with a 23% coefficient of
variation this experiment **cannot resolve** a difference of the size it was looking for, so this is
"did not resolve", not "no effect".
**The larger result is the control's**, which overturned GMB-136's premise. See §4.

## 1. What was tested

GMB-136 found `gmesh-configured`'s native-tool share collapsing at the first `Edit` and hypothesised
a cause: every trigger in `GMESH_CONFIGURED_CLAUDE_MD` is a *pre-change* cue, and nothing addresses
"I just changed something, what did I affect". The transcripts cannot settle it — `thinking` blocks
are present but **encrypted** (`"thinking": ""` plus a signature), so the model's reasoning is not
readable and no number of re-runs makes it readable. The hypothesis needed an intervention.

One sentence was added, immediately after the doc's `find_references` bullet:

> After you change a symbol, "what did I just affect?" is the same question as "what would this
> affect?" — reach for `find_references`/`find_callers` on the symbol you edited rather than
> re-grepping its name to check the blast radius.

+243 bytes (12,805 → 13,048). Built in a `git worktree`, never in the main checkout — this repo has
an incident behind that rule. The main checkout was verified free of the edit afterwards.

**Arms:** control = 3 arms as shipped; treatment = `gmesh-configured` only, since `baseline` and
`serena` never read this document and paying for them would prove nothing.
**Metric:** native/total share of search calls *after* the first `Edit`. Not tokens — tokens are the
symptom, tool choice is the thing under test.

The cue targeted post-edit tool choice and post-edit tool choice did not move.

## 3. Why "worse" cannot be claimed either

Everything else looks worse in treatment — more turns, more tokens, more greps, an oracle failure.
None of it survives a power check.

- Historical spread for `gmesh-configured` on this task: **n=57 runs, mean 15.1 turns, sd 3.5, CV 23%**.
- SE of a 3-vs-3 difference at that sd: **2.86 turns**. Observed difference 5.0 turns = **1.75 SE**. Not significant.
- Placed against the historical mean, it is the **control** that is anomalous: control 10.7 is
  **−2.19 SE** from 15.1, treatment 15.7 is **+0.28 SE**. The treatment side is unremarkable; the
  control side got a lucky draw.

Reps needed at 80% power, given sd 3.5:

| difference to detect | reps per side |
|---|---|
| 7 turns | 4 |
| 5 turns | 8 |
| 3 turns | 22 |

Three per side buys the right to say the hypothesis was not supported on its own metric. It does not
buy a direction on anything else. The single oracle failure is n=1 and carries nothing.

## 4. The control's own result, which is bigger than the A/B

The control run is also the **third independent sweep** of this task, and it contradicts the premise
GMB-136 was opened on — that this task is where g-mesh behaves badly:

| sweep | `gmesh-configured` turns | `baseline` turns |
|---|---|---|
| 2026-08-23 | 14, 12, 17 | 13, 13, 17 |
| 2026-08-24 09:33 | 23, 25, 16 | 10, 11, 12 |
| 2026-08-24 17:03 (this control) | **8, 11, 13** | 18, 11, 18 |

In this sweep g-mesh is the **cheapest and fastest arm of the three**, oracle 3/3. The "21.3 turns
against baseline's 11.5" that made this task look like g-mesh's worst was one sweep's draw.

Over the whole history, **18 sweeps where both arms ran and passed**:

| | g-mesh | baseline | difference |
|---|---|---|---|
| mean tokens per sweep | 550,332 | 374,456 | **+47.0%** |
| mean turns per sweep | 15.3 | 14.2 | **+8.1%** |

Turns are near-parity. The token gap is **175,876**, and the static prefix premium (8,762/turn ×
15.3 turns) accounts for **134,351 of it — 76%**.

**So GMB-136's framing was backwards.** Its description says "It is not the prefix tax - it is the
arm taking 21.3 turns against baseline's 11.5". Measured over 18 sweeps instead of one, it *is*
mostly the prefix tax, and the turn gap is 8%, not 85%.

## 5. What still stands from GMB-136

The phase shift itself, across all three sweeps:

| sweep | pre-edit native | post-edit native | post-edit greps |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2026-08-23 | 64% | 0% | 10 |
| 2026-08-24 09:33 | 29% | 3% | 15 |
| 2026-08-24 17:03 | 29% | 13% | 0 |

Post-edit native share is low in every sweep. But the **grep storm is not reproducible** — 10, 15,
then zero — so "collapse into grep" cannot be offered as a mechanism for the token gap. It is a real
observation about tool choice with, so far, no demonstrated cost attached.

## 6. What to do next, and what not to

**Do not ship the sentence.** It failed on its own metric, and
`v0.20.0-guidance-doc-pays-for-itself-findings.md` is the standing precedent that wording changes
here are behavioural changes.

**Do not re-run this A/B at n=3.** At 8 reps a side it could resolve a 5-turn difference; at 3 it
cannot resolve anything, and the cost is entirely wasted on noise.

**Stop using this task as evidence for anything at n=3.** CV 23% on turns and 33% on tokens means a
three-rep sweep of it produces a headline that reverses between runs — as it did, twice, inside 24
hours. Any claim resting on it needs the paired-across-sweeps view in §4, not a single sweep.
