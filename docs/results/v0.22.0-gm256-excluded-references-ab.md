# GM-256 A/B: the disclosure ships, its benefit is unproven

**Date:** 2026-08-25
**Verdict:** **did not resolve.** Only 3 of 10 runs a side reached the tool the field lives on, so
the comparison had three observations per arm. The change ships on correctness grounds, not on this.

**Runs:** treatment `gmb-gm256/results/token-economy/2026-08-25T09-31-11-864Z.json`, control
`…09-44-12-410Z.json`. 2 tasks × 5 reps, `gmesh-configured` only, g-mesh 2.12.0 both sides.

## Arms were proven different, twice

The lesson GM-237 paid for: "the binaries differ" is not the check.

- The hint string is present in the treatment binary and absent from the control (`strings | grep -c`).
- And, decisively, **in the run**: 3 treatment transcripts contain `excludedReferences`, 0 control
  transcripts do. Both arms report version 2.12.0, so the new `gmeshVersion` field cannot separate
  them — the transcripts can.

A third check caught a trap before it cost anything. The first hand probe of the treatment binary
returned a page with no `excludedReferences` on the exact symbol the feature was built for. The
cause was not the code: `mcp-shim` had attached to a daemon the *control* binary started moments
earlier. After `g-mesh stop`, the same query returned `excludedReferences: {count: 2}` naming
`tests/lifecycle.test.ts` and `tests/ownership.test.ts` — the two files the benchmark's agent had
gone and grepped for. This is GM-237's failure mode in a new place: two arms that are secretly one.

## The result, such as it is

Greps issued *after* a `find_callers`/`find_callees` call, in the runs that made one:

| side | runs that called it | greps after | mean |
|---|---|---|---|
| control | 3 | 11 | 3.7 |
| treatment | 3 | 7 | 2.3 |

Whole-run totals over all 10 reps a side, which move the other way:

| side | mean turns | mean tokens | total greps | oracle |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| control | 10.3 | 344,895 | 23 | 7/10 |
| treatment | 11.0 | 364,693 | 36 | 8/10 |

Three observations per arm on the targeted metric, and a whole-run reading that disagrees with it.
Nothing here supports a claim in either direction.

## Why the experiment was too weak, which was an authoring error

Both faults are in the acceptance criteria I wrote for GM-256, not in the run.

**`tt-references-requiretask` cannot exercise the field at all.** The agent solves it with
`find_references` — and `find_references` never carries `excludedReferences`, correctly, because it
already returns every usage kind and excludes nothing. Naming that task as an A/B target was a
mistake: five of the ten reps a side were incapable of showing an effect. The greps that follow
`find_references` there are the separate re-verification habit (GMB-142 §4), which this change does
not address and was never meant to.

**The other task fires `find_callers` in only about 60% of reps.** `tt-implement-release-cancelled-task-bug`
reaches it in 3 of 5 reps a side, which is where the n=3 comes from.

**What a valid attempt needs.** A task that calls `find_callers` by construction — the registry
already has `ex-find-callers-mutateelement` and `ex-find-callees-mutateelement`, which exist for
exactly this — after first confirming the anchor actually has excluded `REFERENCES` edges in that
corpus, since the field is silent when it does not. With ~all runs firing the tool, 8 reps a side is
a real 8 rather than a nominal one.

## Why it ships anyway

Not on measured benefit — there is none to report. On correctness: `find_callers` was returning a
page that said `hasMore: false` while omitting usages, and one measured agent went and grepped them
up because the answer was wrong to look complete. Disclosing the gap is right whether or not it
changes what the agent does next, and it costs nothing to be right here: **0 bytes when nothing is
excluded**, 238 bytes when something is.

Set against the standing record, this is the third intervention in this area and the third without a
demonstrated behavioural effect — GM-237 (a provenance field, null at 15 reps a side), GMB-139 (a
sentence in the guidance doc, target metric unmoved), and now this one, which did not get enough
observations to say. The pattern worth noticing is that none of the three has yet been given a fair
test at adequate power on a task that reliably triggers it.
