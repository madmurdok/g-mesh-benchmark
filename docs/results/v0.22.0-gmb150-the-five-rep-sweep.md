# GMB-150: five repetitions, and what changed when the estimate got less wrong

**Date:** 2026-08-26
**Run:** 690 records, **all 690 `ok`**, 46 tasks × 3 arms × 5 repetitions. g-mesh 2.12.0,
`claude-sonnet-5`, both corpora, `excalidrawScope: low`, prompt cache warmed, transcripts saved.
**$53.35**, 6h10m of run time across four attempts over two days.

**Records:** `results/token-economy/2026-08-26T12-29-26-546Z.json` plus the earlier
`2026-08-25T23-02-04-943Z-partial.json`. The partial holds six tasks completed before an abort; the
final file holds the other forty. One task appears in both — it was re-run whole — and is
de-duplicated by keeping the last five repetitions per (task, arm), which is what the analysis does.

## The headline

Paired per task against baseline, counting only tasks where both arms ran ok and passed the oracle:

| arm | tasks | tokens/task | vs baseline | median per-task Δ | turns/task | vs baseline |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `gmesh-configured` | 46 | 116,145 | **+10.0%** | **+30.0%** | 4.7 | **−34.2%** |
| `serena-configured` | 46 | 157,212 | +49.0% | +40.1% | 8.8 | +23.7% |
| `baseline` | 46 | 105,539 | — | — | 7.1 | — |

Oracle pass: **g-mesh 226/230 (98.3%)**, baseline 222/230 (96.5%), Serena 221/230 (96.1%).
Spend: g-mesh $16.43, baseline $16.58, Serena $20.34.

Per-run medians: g-mesh 71,417 tokens over 3.0 turns, baseline 55,042 over 4.0, Serena 78,145 over
6.0. Static prefix, turn-1 of warm runs: g-mesh 22,066, baseline 12,943 — a premium of **9,123 per
turn**.

## Why the mean improved while the doc got dearer

The 3-rep sweep of 2026-08-24 reported **+22.8%**. This one reports **+10.0%**, and it ran with a
*more* expensive guidance doc. That looks backwards, and it is one task.

Biggest movers against the 3-rep sweep, per-task Δ of g-mesh against baseline:

| task | category | 3-rep Δ | 5-rep Δ | shift |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `tt-implement-release-cancelled-task-bug` | implementation | **+194%** | **+15%** | **−180** |
| `ex-namespace-import-laserpointer-plerp` | lookup | +188% | +97% | −91 |
| `tt-deps-incoming-db-connection` | lookup | +84% | +6% | −78 |
| `ex-outline-types-vs-i18n` | lookup | +69% | +2% | −67 |
| … | | | | |
| `ex-implement-mutateelement-elbow-zero-position` | implementation | +6% | +103% | +98 |
| `tt-semantic-dedupe-prefix-collision` | semantic-search | +28% | +94% | +65 |

The top row is the whole story. `tt-implement-release-cancelled-task-bug` is the noisiest task in
the corpus — CV 23% on turns, 33% on tokens, documented in GMB-141 and GMB-143 as having reversed
its own headline twice inside 24 hours — and it is also the largest by absolute tokens, so it
dominates a sum of per-task means. At three repetitions it drew badly. At five it regressed toward
its own average.

**So the tool did not get 12 points better. The estimate got less wrong.** That is the whole reason
this sweep was run at five, and it is worth stating plainly: the previous headline was carrying one
task's bad draw.

## Why the median moved the other way

26.9% → **30.0%**, and this one *is* the resync.

The guidance doc was 767 bytes behind g-mesh's shipped snippet from 2026-08-21 until 2026-08-26
(GMB-148). Syncing it took the prefix premium from 8,762 to **9,123** tokens per turn. At the median
3 turns that is **+1,083 tokens**, which against a 55,042-token median baseline run is **+2.0
points**.

Observed shift: +3.1 points. So the resync accounts for roughly two thirds of it. **The remaining
third is unexplained and is left that way** — candidates are the task added by GMB-141, the bytes
GM-256 adds to some responses, and ordinary variation, and nothing here distinguishes them.

## The divergence is the finding

At three repetitions mean and median agreed (+22.8% / +26.9%). At five they do not (+10.0% / +30.0%),
and the gap is structural rather than noise:

- **The median says what a typical task costs.** A per-turn prefix of 9,123 tokens lands on every
  task, and on a small lookup that is a large fraction of a short run. Most tasks in this registry
  are small.
- **The mean says what the registry costs in total**, and is set by the few large tasks — where
  g-mesh's turn saving (−34.2%) has enough turns to work against.

Quote whichever answers the question being asked, and never the pair as though they measured the
same thing. If the question is "what will this cost me on a typical lookup", the answer is +30%. If
it is "what does a whole sweep of work cost", it is +10%.

## What the run itself cost to get

Four attempts over two days. Three aborted, every one on serena's MCP handshake failing both retry
attempts.

**serena was never broken** — started standalone it resolves and reports 1.7.1.dev0. The machine was
the problem: load averaged **144–160** during the failing attempts, driven by macOS system services
(`trustd`, six XProtect processes, `launchctl dumpstate`, `WindowServer`) *plus serena's own
TypeScript language servers*, which were observed at 154% and 94% CPU. The serena arm contributes
materially to the load that then kills its own handshake.

The attempt that finished did two things differently: it waited until **all three** load averages
were low rather than just the one-minute figure — an earlier attempt started at 1-min 4.5 while
15-min was 60, i.e. in a trough between spikes, and hit full load minutes later — and it killed
leftover serena processes first, since serena's `SessionEnd` cleanup hook is cancelled on every
failure (`Hook cancelled` appears in all three aborts) and the leftovers block the next start.

## `durationMs` from this sweep is unusable

Runs are split across windows of load 5 and load 150+, and **nothing in the record says which**. The
figures are in the file because the harness always writes them; they should not be read, and no
timing conclusion should be drawn from this sweep. A run record carries `corpusRevision`,
`serenaRevision` and now `gmeshVersion`, but not the machine's state — which is the one thing that
would make a duration comparable.
